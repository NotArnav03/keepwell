# Q3: How does a revise (plan change) approval work on the sandbox?
Owner: Om (issue #31) · Date: 2026-11-12 · **Answered on the sandbox, with two things we could not observe (see "Not observed")**

## Answer
**A revise needs the buyer to approve it on PayPal's own page, and the change shows on the subscription only after that approval.** The engine's design (send the revise, show "Waiting for your approval in PayPal…", open the link in a popup, read the subscription when the buyer returns) matches what PayPal does. What the mock did not know:
1. **The approve link.** `POST /v1/billing/subscriptions/{id}/revise` with `{plan_id, application_context: {return_url, cancel_url}}` answers **200** with the subscription and `links`: `approve` (GET `https://www.sandbox.paypal.com/webapps/billing/subscriptions/update?ba_token=BA-…`), plus `edit`, `self`, `cancel`, `suspend`, `capture`. The `rel` is `approve`, the same as for a new subscription, but the URL path is `/subscriptions/update`, not the plain `/subscriptions` page.
2. **Where the buyer lands.** After "Agree & Subscribe" PayPal redirects to the `return_url` with **`subscription_id`, `ba_token` and `token`** appended. If the `return_url` already has a query string, PayPal appends with `&` and keeps ours: `…/v1/revise/return?action=0199-test&subscription_id=I-…&ba_token=BA-…&token=…`. So `/v1/revise/return?action=<id>` works as built (its zod query ignores the extra keys). The approval page showed the new price ($2.00 USD for the first revise, $1.00 for the second) before the buyer agreed.
3. **When the change applies.** Reading the subscription with `?fields=plan` **before** approval still showed the old plan and price (checked on the second revise: plan unchanged until the buyer agreed). **After** approval it showed the new plan and price at once (`fixed_price` 2.0, then back to 1.0 after the second revise), with **`next_billing_time` unchanged** (2026-11-13T10:00Z) and status ACTIVE. So the new plan is on the subscription immediately and is charged from the next billing; that charge falls after this spike.
4. **The revise response echoes the new `plan_id`** straight away (`plan_overridden` false), although the subscription itself still carries the old plan until approval. **Do not read the revise response as confirmation.** Confirm by reading the subscription after the buyer returns.

## The approval-page loop
The loop seen in the Q4 spike (back to login after "Agree and Subscribe") **did not happen** here: three approvals (one new subscription and two revises) completed in one Chrome session where the buyer was already signed in. Judge Mode's "approve as the sandbox buyer" step should still tell judges to sign in to the buyer first, and to expect a login if the session is new.

## Not observed
- **Webhook for a revise: none seen.** A webhook was registered on the app before the revises and read back with `GET /v1/notifications/webhooks-events` for about five minutes afterwards. Neither revise produced any event (no `BILLING.SUBSCRIPTION.UPDATED`). Events for a *new* subscription arrived normally in the same window (`CREATED`, then `ACTIVATED` and `PAYMENT.SALE.COMPLETED` about a minute later; see `q7/95-q7-events.json`). So **a revise must not depend on a webhook**: the engine has to read the subscription when the buyer returns and on its sweep, which is how it works.
- The first charge at the new price (2026-11-13T10:00Z) was not waited for.

## Impact on the design
No code change needed. Notes for the gateway and the engine: do not trust the revise response; confirm from the subscription read; the return URL carries extra query keys; a revise gives no webhook, so the due-work sweep is the safety net.
