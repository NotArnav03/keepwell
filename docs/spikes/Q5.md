# Q5: Can a real renewal failure be forced in sandbox?
Owner: Om · Date: 2026-11-04

## Answer (one paragraph)
**Yes.** A subscription on a `DAY` plan whose buyer has no working funding source fails its renewals for real: PayPal counted **3 failed payments**, left **$3.00 outstanding**, and **suspended the subscription on its own** at the plan's `payment_failure_threshold` of 3. No simulation was needed. The catch is time: a daily plan gives one attempt per day, so the failing state took about two days to reach, and a threshold of 3 means a subscription sits in "failed but still retrying" for only a day or two before PayPal suspends it. Failed attempts do **not** appear in `GET /v1/billing/subscriptions/{id}/transactions` (it still listed only the first successful payment), so failures have to be read from the subscription's `billing_info` (`failed_payments_count`, `outstanding_balance`, `status`).

## Evidence (requests and responses with secrets removed; PayPal-Debug-Id values)
Sandbox, US business and personal test accounts. Plan `P-0RV456230L274172RNLRYXYQ`: `$1.00 / DAY`, `auto_bill_outstanding: true`, `payment_failure_threshold: 3`. Subscription `I-4A72UBGNESWR`, approved on 2026-10-31 and paid with a Visa (first payment `COMPLETED` at 2026-10-31T04:35:11Z). The buyer was created with a $0 PayPal balance, then the card and the bank account were removed from the buyer's wallet by hand on 2026-11-01, before that day's renewal was processed.

**Not observed, so not claimed:**
- When each of the three failures happened, and the failure reason code. We did not sample the subscription between failures. After suspension `billing_info.last_failed_payment` was `null`, so whether it is filled in during the retry window is untested. The reason code may only arrive in the `BILLING.SUBSCRIPTION.PAYMENT.FAILED` webhook body (the sandbox webhook was not registered for this test).
- Which of the two removals (card or bank) mattered. Both were removed.
- What happens when the buyer adds funding again after suspension (reactivation, and capture of the $3.00 outstanding). That is the recovery half of the story and is still untested against real sandbox.

## Impact on the design (link the ADR if one was needed)
- **The SIMULATED fallback is not needed for the failure itself.** ARCHITECTURE §6.17 and §8.3 can describe a real failure. It is still needed for **live Judge Mode sessions**: nobody can wait two days for a judge's click, so the "simulate a failed payment" button stays, badged `SIMULATED`.
- **Keep a real failing subscription available for the demo and the video.** Because suspension arrives after `payment_failure_threshold` failures, create the demo plan with a **higher threshold** (the plans API accepts a larger number; this was not tested here, so verify on the first plan) so a pooled subscription stays in "failed, retrying" for days instead of one. Suggested to M1 (pool) and M3 (Judge Mode).
- **Recovery reads failures from `billing_info`, as the design already says.** Webhooks are hints (design law 6), and the transactions list does not show failed attempts. The sync should compare `failed_payments_count` and `outstanding_balance` against our snapshot. M1 should confirm `SubscriptionSnapshot.lastFailure` copes with `last_failed_payment: null` after a real failure (recovery then falls back to the `generic` playbook when no reason code is known).
- The mock already models this (`payment_failure_threshold` suspends after that many failed renewals, `mock.state.renew(id, "PAYER_CANNOT_PAY")`), which matches this finding. No mock change.
- No ADR: this resolves an open question rather than changing the design.
