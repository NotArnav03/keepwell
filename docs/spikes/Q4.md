# Q4: Does the 10-day rule for price overrides behave the same in sandbox?
Owner: Ishan (run by Om, who had a working sandbox buyer) · Date: 2026-11-07, closed 2026-11-09 · **Answered; the outside-window control was not run, see "Still to confirm"**

## Answer (one paragraph)
**Inside the 10-day window the override is accepted and does not change the subscription.** On an ACTIVE `$1.00/DAY` subscription whose next billing time was `2026-11-08T10:00:00Z` (well under 10 days away), PayPal answered `PATCH /v1/billing/subscriptions/{id}` with **204 No Content** for `replace /plan/billing_cycles/@sequence==1/pricing_scheme/fixed_price` = `0.50`, and an immediate `GET ...?fields=plan` still showed `fixed_price` `1.0`. There was no error, so a client cannot tell from the response that the override will not apply: this matches the design rule that Keepwell must never offer a price discount when the next billing is under 10 days away (the policy checks it before the action is created). **The next real charge confirmed it:** on 2026-11-08T13:18Z the subscription was billed **$1.00**, not $0.50 (transaction `26A32981GJ289692R`, COMPLETED), and `fixed_price` still read 1.0 after it. **Two details of the endpoint that the gateway must respect:** (1) the patchable path is `.../pricing_scheme/fixed_price` with the Money object as `value`; patching `.../pricing_scheme` with a whole scheme is rejected with **400 `INVALID_PATCH_PATH` "Specified field cannot be patched"**; (2) `fields=plan` must be requested on the GET to see the price at all (the default response has no `plan` block).

## Still to confirm
(The 8 Nov charge was the first item here; it is now answered above: $1.00.)
1. **The outside-window case was not tested.** A control (a monthly plan, next billing a month away, PATCH accepted and applied) needed a second approved subscription, and the sandbox approval page looped back to login in two browsers (normal and private) and never approved. So "a PATCH more than 10 days before billing does apply" rests on PayPal's documentation, not on a sandbox run. A teammate with a working approval flow can run it: create a monthly plan, approve a subscription, PATCH `fixed_price`, read it back.

## Impact on the design
- **No policy change so far.** The 10-day rule in `packages/policy` (`rules.ts`) stays as it is; the sandbox does not contradict it.
- **Gateway note:** build the PATCH with the exact `.../pricing_scheme/fixed_price` path and read the result with `fields=plan`. Because PayPal answers 204 even when it will not apply the change, the engine must not treat 204 as proof: it should re-GET and compare, as it already does for other mutations (design law: webhooks and responses are hints).
