# Q2: After suspend → activate, how does billing resume?
Owner: Arnav · Dates: 2026-11-01 (short pause), 2026-11-02 (pause across one billing date) · several billing dates: running, resumes after 2026-11-04T10:00Z

## Answer (one paragraph)
**Short pause (no billing date passes while suspended): nothing changes.** Suspending, then activating 10 seconds later, charged nothing at activation or in the minute after it. `next_billing_time` came back exactly as before (`2026-11-02T10:00:00Z`), `outstanding_balance` stayed `0.0` and `cycles_completed` stayed 1. **While SUSPENDED, PayPal leaves `next_billing_time` out of the subscription entirely**, and activation brings it back. **Pause across a billing date: PayPal charges the missed cycle right after activation, so a pause delays a payment rather than skipping it.** Suspended at 11:41Z on 1 Nov, before the 2 Nov 10:00Z billing time, and activated at about 19:07Z on 2 Nov. Activation itself charged nothing and restored the old, already-past `next_billing_time`. **Three minutes later (19:10:10Z) PayPal charged one $1.00 cycle as an ordinary payment.** `cycles_completed` went to 2 and `outstanding_balance` stayed 0. Billing then went on at the original time of day (next: 3 Nov 10:00Z). **Still open:** a pause across several billing dates, to see whether there is one catch-up charge or one per missed date. The subscription is suspended again before 3 Nov 10:00Z.

Side findings:
- **The first `next_billing_time` is not approval + 1 day.** The subscription was approved at 11:09Z and the next billing time is 10:00:00Z the next day: PayPal bills at a fixed time of day, not at the minute of approval. Code and copy must not assume "exactly one interval after the start".
- `outstanding_balance.value` comes back as `"0.0"`, like `last_payment` (Q1's `"1.0"`). `toMoney()` already normalises both.
- The first sandbox calls timed out (10 s) several times in a row, then worked. The gateway's retries covered single timeouts; the spike's polling loop gave up after four in a row.

## Impact on the design (link the ADR if one was needed)
- **This changes the design: an ADR follows once the several-dates run is in.** A pause offer that resumes after a billing date doesn't save the subscriber that payment: they're charged for it minutes after resuming. The offer card and policy (M2) must not say "skip a month". Options for the ADR:
  - resume on a billing date, so the catch-up charge is that day's normal payment;
  - pair the pause with a discount for the missed cycle;
  - word the offer honestly as a delay.
- The pause saga takes the resume date from our `scheduled_resumes`, never from PayPal, so the missing `next_billing_time` while suspended changes nothing in the engine. Anything that shows "next charge" for a paused subscription must say "paused" instead of reading PayPal's field.
- paypal-mock now hides `next_billing_time` while suspended and restores it on activation (tested). After a pause across a billing date the mock still skips the missed cycle, which is wrong. It changes once the several-dates run shows whether PayPal charges once or per missed date.
