# Q2: After suspend → activate, how does billing resume?
Owner: Arnav · Dates: 2026-11-01 (short pause), 2026-11-02 (pause across one billing date), 2026-11-03 to 05 (pause across three billing dates) · **Answered**

## Answer (one paragraph)
**Short pause (no billing date passes while suspended): nothing changes.** Suspending, then activating 10 seconds later, charged nothing at activation or in the minute after it. `next_billing_time` came back exactly as before (`2026-11-02T10:00:00Z`), `outstanding_balance` stayed `0.0` and `cycles_completed` stayed 1. **While SUSPENDED, PayPal leaves `next_billing_time` out of the subscription entirely**, and activation brings it back. **Pause across a billing date: PayPal charges the missed cycle right after activation, so a pause delays a payment rather than skipping it.** Suspended at 11:41Z on 1 Nov, before the 2 Nov 10:00Z billing time, and activated at about 19:07Z on 2 Nov. Activation itself charged nothing and restored the old, already-past `next_billing_time`. **Three minutes later (19:10:10Z) PayPal charged one $1.00 cycle as an ordinary payment.** `cycles_completed` went to 2 and `outstanding_balance` stayed 0. Billing then went on at the original time of day (next: 3 Nov 10:00Z). **Pause across several billing dates: one catch-up charge, never one per missed date.** Suspended at 06:33Z on 3 Nov, before the 3 Nov 10:00Z billing time, so three billing times passed (3, 4 and 5 Nov at 10:00Z), and activated at about 13:50Z on 5 Nov. Activation charged nothing and set `next_billing_time` to **5 Nov 10:00Z, the latest billing time that had passed**, not the 3 Nov one from before the pause. PayPal charged one $1.00 at 18:31Z (4.7 hours later, against 3 minutes in the first run), `cycles_completed` went from 2 to 3, and billing went on as normal (next: 6 Nov 10:00Z). The 3 and 4 Nov cycles were never billed. **So the rule is: on activation PayPal bills the cycle of the latest billing time that has passed, once and some time later; every earlier missed cycle is skipped.** A pause that ends on a billing date skips exactly the payments before that date.

Side findings:
- **The first `next_billing_time` is not approval + 1 day.** The subscription was approved at 11:09Z and the next billing time is 10:00:00Z the next day: PayPal bills at a fixed time of day, not at the minute of approval. Code and copy must not assume "exactly one interval after the start".
- `outstanding_balance.value` comes back as `"0.0"`, like `last_payment` (Q1's `"1.0"`). `toMoney()` already normalises both.
- The first sandbox calls timed out (10 s) several times in a row, then worked. The gateway's retries covered single timeouts; the spike's polling loop gave up after four in a row.

## Impact on the design (link the ADR if one was needed)
- **ADR [0001](../adr/0001-pause-resumes-on-a-billing-date.md): a pause ends on a billing date.** The policy already sets `resumesOn = nextBilling + c cycles`, and the resume runs at that time or after it. So the catch-up charge is that day's normal payment, and "pause for c cycles" skips exactly c payments. That makes the offer card's "pause for N billing cycles, resuming <date>" true as written. What the ADR rules out is a resume *before* the billing time on its date, which would be billed for the previous cycle and then again hours later.
- **Judge Mode's TIME SHIFT (#141)** resumes early. Straight after a pause no billing time has passed, so PayPal charges nothing, the same as the short-pause case. If one has passed, the judge's pool buyer is charged one cycle of sandbox money, which is honest and harmless.
- **The pool's restore** (activate on return) can likewise bill the pool buyer one sandbox cycle. That's fine.
- The pause saga takes the resume date from our `scheduled_resumes`, never from PayPal, so the missing `next_billing_time` while suspended changes nothing in the engine. Anything that shows "next charge" for a paused subscription must say "paused" instead of reading PayPal's field. Right after a late activation, PayPal's `next_billing_time` is in the past until the catch-up charge lands. Show it as "due now", not as a date that has gone by.
- **paypal-mock** now follows both runs: it hides `next_billing_time` while suspended; on activation it brings back the same one after a short pause, or the latest one that passed after a long pause, and charges nothing then. `renew()` stands for PayPal's later charge. Tested in `server.test.ts`.
